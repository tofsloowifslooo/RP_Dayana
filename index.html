02 / 15
Research Question

This is the starting point.

How do young Kazakh Muslims account for aspects of Islamic tradition that are publicly contested in Kazakhstan?

The question shifts attention from simply asking what is contested to asking how people themselves make sense of it.

03 / 15
Clarifying the Question

What do I mean by “account”?

Explain

How participants explain what a religious practice means to them.

Interpret

How they understand practices, traditions, and disagreements.

Negotiate

How they respond when different interpretations meet.

I am not assuming that participants must defend or justify their beliefs.

Context

Islam as a “way of life”

The revival of Islam is not only about religious institutions.

Omelicheva describes processes of individualization and intimization of Islam: religious ideas can become connected to personal ethics, everyday choices, and ways of living.

If religion becomes part of everyday life, people also have to interpret what it means in their own circumstances.

Omelicheva (2011)

When religion meets national identity

What does it mean to be Muslim?
What does it mean to be Kazakh?

Sikhimbayeva and Sadvokassova examine the relationship between Islam and ethnonational identity in Kazakhstan.

Their analysis shows that religious and national identities can overlap, reinforce one another, or become interpreted as being in tension.

The important question is: how do people themselves understand this relationship?

Analytical Framework

Three sensitizing concepts

01

Meaning-making

How do participants understand religious practices, experiences, and disagreements?

02

Religious authority

Whose interpretation is treated as credible? Family, imam, scripture, online teacher, or personal experience?

03

Boundary-making

How are boundaries such as Islamic / un-Islamic, Kazakh / foreign, traditional / non-traditional constructed?

These are sensitizing concepts, not predetermined findings.

Contribution

What could this study add?

Empirical

A closer account of how young Kazakh Muslims interpret and explain contested aspects of religious tradition.

Conceptual

Connecting broader processes such as individualization and public religion with micro-level meaning-making.

Scope

The aim is not to represent all young Kazakh Muslims, but to illuminate particular participants' experiences and interpretations.

Final Reflection

The literature tells me what the terrain looks like.

My research asks how people experience and interpret that terrain.

AI helped me understand the difference between studying a phenomenon and studying how people make sense of that phenomenon.

What I still need to work out: how to recognize participant meanings without imposing my own.
